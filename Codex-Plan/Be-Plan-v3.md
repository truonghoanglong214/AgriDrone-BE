3. Register content of Capstone Project

3.1.1. English: AgriDrone: Smart Dragon Fruit Farm Management System Using Drone and AI

Abbreviation: AgriDrone
(*) 3.2. Main proposal content (including result and product)
a) Context:
Dragon-fruit orchards need accurate farm maps, pole counts and preliminary disease screening. AgriDrone offers two paid drone surveys. The first Manager-approved paid survey establishes the farm's Baseline Map and initial Digital Plant Profiles; later paid surveys track changes over time. Customers pay one fixed deposit before flight and the remaining balance before results are released. Claims require auditable evidence.
GNSS drift complicates matching physical poles across repeat surveys, especially near farm boundaries. Original imagery, GNSS logs, plant identities and report versions are retained for comparison, review and dispute handling.
b) Proposed Solutions:
The MVP has six components (S1-S6) with two paid services and no free Baseline survey. A Follow-up Survey is a new paid Order for an existing farm, not a free re-flight. The system also reports monthly service revenue and cash flow; detailed rules appear in the SRS.
• S1 – Service and onboarding: Public Applicants request Map Survey (map, pole count/positions) or Map + Disease Screening (Combo: Map plus Manager-reviewed suspected diseases). SystemAdmin approves onboarding, provisions Tenant/Farm/Order and assigns a Manager. An existing TenantOwner books a Follow-up for an existing Farm with an approved Baseline through a new Order, without creating duplicate Tenant or Farm records. Disease screening is not sold alone.
• S2 – Pricing, payment and Baseline Map: each new Order (first or Follow-up) snapshots its ServiceType, applicable PricePerPole (Map or Combo) and shared fixed DepositAmount. The deposit is paid before flight; Total = verified billable poles inside FarmBoundary × PricePerPole, and the deposit is credited once against the online balance paid after flight and before release. The first Manager-approved paid map becomes the farm's versioned Baseline, linked to Order and Mission. Follow-ups reference that Baseline without overwriting it. A Re-flight corrects inadequate imagery in the same Order at no extra service charge, subject to safe and authorized operations. No discounts or separate Follow-up price tier.
• S3 – AI and review: both services map/count poles and match later detections to Baseline pole identities using GNSS and orchard row/column; near/outside-boundary detections and uncertain matches are flagged NeedsReview. Combo additionally screens 2–3 team-labelled suspected diseases, proposes Disease Zones and links referenced advice. SystemManager reviews all proposed results before publication. No severity grading or certified diagnosis.
• S4 – Digital Plant Profile and monitoring: the approved first Baseline creates one active profile per verified physical pole. Each Follow-up adds dated, evidence-linked observations to the same PlantProfileId for matched poles, without replacing history. Newly detected poles or absent poles are flagged NeedsReview; a new profile, retirement or replacement requires later-survey evidence and SystemManager approval, and prior profiles remain inactive/retired. Approved Combo findings enter disease history; Map-only surveys update mapping data without assuming disease clearance.
• S5 – Delivery: Web and minimal online-only Manager Mobile App for checklists, notes and imagery/GNSS uploads. Once Manager-reviewed results and the post-flight balance are complete, TenantOwner views the current Baseline, each paid Follow-up, per-pole history, comparison reports and retained map/report versions. Repeat flights are distinguished from free quality Re-flights within an Order.
• S6 – Complaints, refunds and revenue: after full payment and result unlock, TenantOwner may claim within 7 calendar days of the unlock notification, with dated evidence of Map errors or Combo disease-result errors. SystemManager verifies imagery, GNSS, AI outputs and report versions. SystemAdmin and TenantOwner agree an evidence-based refund amount according to the error's impact; cumulative refunds cannot exceed amounts actually received for that Order. Refunds are manual bank transfers with proof and audit history. Incorrect reports/Baselines are withdrawn and replaced by reviewed versions. Admin dashboard and Excel summarize monthly service value, payments, refunds, net cash and outstanding balances by service.
MVP scope (S1-S6): paid Map/Combo, shared pre-flight deposit and online post-flight balance; first approved paid map as versioned Baseline; separately charged Follow-up Orders on existing Farms; persistent plant identities and approved observations; Combo disease screening; negotiated evidence-based refunds; monthly Revenue Dashboard and Excel export.
Excluded: Harvest Readiness, expert-certified diagnosis, disease-only flights, tenant-operated or autonomous flights, in-flight AI, mandatory RTK, offline mode, spraying, workforce/yield management, other crops and diseases outside the 2–3 selected classes.
c) Functional Requirements:
Actors: Public Applicant, SystemAdmin, SystemManager and TenantOwner; AI is internal. Each first or Follow-up Order snapshots ServiceType, PricePerPole and DepositAmount. The farm's approved Baseline references source Order/Mission/version; subsequent Orders reference the same Farm and Baseline. Re-flights share their original Order.
• Public Applicant: view both services, their per-pole prices and the deposit amount; submit a service request and follow its status and decision with a tracking code.
• SystemAdmin: approve onboarding requests; manage service rates, the deposit amount, staff and drones; decide claims, agree refund amounts with TenantOwners and record refund transfers; view the monthly revenue dashboard and export filtered Excel reports.
• SystemManager: fly after deposit, repeat inadequate capture under the same Order at no added service charge, and review maps/boundaries/positions. Approve the first paid map as Baseline; match Follow-up poles to existing PlantProfileIds, resolve NeedsReview and approve new/retired/replaced poles and observations. Review Combo suspected diseases, claims and corrected report versions.
• TenantOwner: select Map or Combo for a first survey or book a new paid Follow-up on an existing Farm; pay the shared deposit before flight and the balance online before viewing results. Track Baseline/Follow-up comparisons and per-pole histories; report plant changes with evidence; claim within 7 days after result unlock and track agreed refunds.
• AI module: detect/map/count poles, propose Baseline identities, match Follow-up observations against earlier approved pole records using GNSS+grid, and flag uncertain, new, missing or boundary-adjacent poles for review. For Combo, propose suspected disease locations and zones. No automatic creation/retirement of Plant Profiles or inference that Map-only results mean disease-free; no severity, Harvest Readiness or certified diagnosis.
Acceptance: (1) first and Follow-up Orders snapshot service price and shared deposit; (2) no free Baseline flight, and a Follow-up creates a new paid Order without duplicate Tenant/Farm; (3) first approved map becomes the versioned Baseline, while Follow-ups only reference it; (4) total = verified in-boundary poles × snapshotted rate, deposit credited once; (5) quality Re-flights stay within the same Order without additional service fees; (6) one active PlantProfileId per verified pole, with dated, evidence-linked observations; (7) new, absent or replaced poles require Manager review; inactive profiles and history remain; (8) Map-only surveys do not clear disease history; (9) results unlock after Manager review and full payment; (10) fully paid Orders permit evidenced claims within 7 days of unlock notice; (11) agreed refunds are audited and capped by net paid amount; (12) corrected reports are versioned; (13) monthly dashboard and Excel reconcile with transactions.
d) Non-Functional Requirements:
• NFR1 – Performance: measure p95 read-API response time (pilot target ≤2 s), image upload time, post-flight review time (pilot target ≤30 min per session) and dataset/hardware limits; targets are provisional.
• NFR2 – AI: measure held-out pole-count error (target ≤10%), RQ1 GNSS-only versus GNSS+grid matching/ID switches on paid repeat surveys and unchanged pole identities, and Combo RQ2 per-class precision/recall/F1 (pilot macro-F1 ≥0.70). Metrics reflect team labels, not certified diagnosis; targets are provisional.
• NFR3 – Security: RBAC and Tenant/Farm data isolation; payments go through the gateway without storing card data; protect media and beneficiary bank details; audit payments, claim evidence, refunds, report corrections and Admin-only financial exports.
• NFR4 – Integrity: test one active versioned Baseline per Farm, only from an approved first paid Order; new Follow-up Orders and separate free quality Re-flights; service/deposit snapshots, payment/idempotency and result lock. Verify persistent pole IDs, no unreviewed creation/retirement/replacement, preserved history, Map-only disease neutrality, 7-day claims after unlock and refund cap.
• NFR5 – Connectivity: the Web and Mobile apps work online only (no offline cache); interrupted uploads must resume or retry safely and show clear error states.
• NFR6 – Usability and safety: measure task completion for field workflows; document pre-flight checklists, failsafe procedures and applicable operational requirements.
• NFR7 – Monthly revenue reporting: the SystemAdmin filters by month/year and service, and the Excel export shows the same figures. Months follow Vietnam time (UTC+7). Service value = verified billable poles × snapshotted PricePerPole, recognized once per Order on its original Manager-approved publication date (not the flight date); corrected publications do not create another sale. Net service revenue = service value − completed refunds of recognized service value, posted on refund completion dates. Overpayment returns and returns of unpublished deposits were not service value and affect cash rather than reducing service revenue again. Net cash = actual deposits + balance receipts − all completed cash refunds, each posted on its transaction/completion date; pending payments/refunds are excluded. The report also shows published Orders, deposits held for active unpublished Orders, refundable excess, separately classified cancellation retention and outstanding balances at month end, without counting the deposit twice. Historical as-of balances and a shared dashboard/Excel query are required; detailed classifications follow Business Rules v3.
e) Theory & Practical:
Theory: object detection and de-duplication, GIS and GNSS/grid matching, visible disease screening, spatial clustering, sourced advisory content, annotation quality and human-in-the-loop review. Disease severity grading is excluded.
Practical: existing drone imagery and GNSS/flight logs are used for field evaluation; additional flights require applicable authorization. All Map and Combo outputs are reviewed by the SystemManager. The team labels 2–3 suspected disease classes from references, without specialist verification. Stack: ASP.NET Core, PostgreSQL/PostGIS, FastAPI, GNSS drone, Web and Mobile apps.

3.3. Research Information
a. Research Problem / Research Question
Problem: GNSS drift causes pole identity errors across repeat flights, and disease symptoms can resemble sunburn or scarring. Team-labelled images support model comparison, not expert diagnosis.
RQ1 – Does GNSS + row/column improve repeat-flight pole matches and reduce ID switches versus GNSS-only, without RTK?
RQ2 – How does the proposed detector locate/classify 2–3 suspected diseases versus a standard detector on held-out team-labelled images?
Hypotheses, not findings: H1 – grid assistance reduces ID switches; H2 – the proposed disease detector raises macro-F1 versus baseline on identical held-out team-labelled data.
b. Research Objectives
• O1: quantify pole-count, RQ1 match and identity-switch errors across repeat flights.
• O2: team-define 2–3 visible disease classes, compare detector precision/recall/F1 and report label uncertainty; omit severity.
• O3: examine sunburn/scarring, lighting, occlusion, GNSS drift, label noise and single-orchard limits.
• O4: measure flight/processing time, boundary cases and review effort.
c. Research Scope & Methodology
Pilot: Existing drone imagery and GNSS records provide initial field data; provenance and usage rights will be reviewed. Additional flights require applicable authorizations. WP2 identifies reference poles and evaluates repeat-survey matching. The team selects 2–3 visible disease candidates using experience and references; members label images using written rules, flag uncertain cases and report pilot counts. Labels are not specialist-verified.
Evaluation: Before augmentation, split the original dataset into Training (70%), Validation (20%) and Testing (10%). Group images by source video/flight session and plant/plot where feasible, ensuring that duplicates, near-duplicate frames and edited versions of the same original image remain within one subset. Apply augmentation only to Training. Use Validation for model tuning and reserve Testing for final evaluation. Check for cross-split duplicates and report image counts per class in each subset. RQ1 compares GNSS-only and GNSS+grid on paired flights (match/ID switches). RQ2 compares the proposed versus standard detector (e.g., YOLO) on identical held-out images/classes: per-class precision/recall/F1 and macro-F1. Metrics measure agreement with team labels, not diagnosis; no severity assessment.
Ablate grid priors and confusing non-disease cases. Run three seeds on identical splits/hardware; report mean/spread and retain dataset versions, annotation decisions, references, scripts and logs. Pilot tests both RQs before integration; external diagnostic validity remains unknown.
d. Expected Scientific Contribution
Expected: reproducible GNSS pole-identity benchmark and image-based disease-detector comparison on team-labelled data. Outcomes are preliminary label-agreement results, not specialist diagnoses; severity is excluded.

Approved operating boundaries: before capture, customer cancellation returns all receipts; after acceptable capture, cancellation solely because the customer changes their mind retains at most the snapshotted deposit, returns excess receipts and does not collect the unpaid balance. Weather/safety rescheduling does not forfeit the deposit; one pre-capture no-show reschedule is free. If the team cannot deliver and the Order is stopped, all receipts are returned. Retained cancellation deposits are reported separately from published service value. .NET owns transactional business state; FastAPI processes imagery/AI behind authenticated/versioned adapters; SePay remains the MVP payment integration. Manager Mobile is online-only with safe upload retry/resume.
